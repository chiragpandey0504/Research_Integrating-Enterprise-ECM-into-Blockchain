## Abstract
Heightened interest in transparency and security within business systems has spurred research on emergent technologies. This study explores integrating Blockchain Technology (BCT) into Engineering Change Management (ECM) via the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), assessing Perceived Usefulness (PU) and Perceived Ease of Use (PEU) through a quantitative survey.

## Keywords
blockchain technology · enterprise integration · engineering change management · technology acceptance model

## Research Questions
1. To what extent is BCT integration perceived to influence an ERP-driven ECM process?  
2. To what extent do users perceive enhanced ease of use in a blockchain-enabled ECM process?

## Data Collection
**Instrument:**  
- 18 questions covering demographics, ERP/ECM/BCT experience, PU, and PEU factors.  
- 7-point Likert Scale (Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree) for PU and PEU responses.

**Sample:**  
- 85 professionals from manufacturing, IT, and education sectors.  
- Experience in ERP and ECM.  
- Recruitment via ASCM Inland Empire, America’s SAP Users’ Group (ASUG), and selected LinkedIn professional groups.  
- Convenience sampling was used, balancing diversity despite the niche participant pool.

## Tools Used
- **Qualtrics** for survey design and data collection  
- **Power BI** for descriptive visualization  
- **R** (packages: `tidyverse`, `psych`, `car`, `caret`) for statistical analysis

## Methodology & Analysis

**1. Descriptive Statistics**  
- Strong mean values observed across PU and PEU items.  
- Higher positive perceptions toward blockchain’s usefulness than seamless ease of use.

**2. Reliability Testing (Cronbach’s Alpha)**  
- PU Items: 0.935 (Excellent consistency)  
- PEU Items: 0.815 (Good consistency)  
✅ Indicates survey items reliably measured their intended constructs.

**3. Correlation Analysis**  
- Positive, significant correlations between PU and PEU variables.  
- Interpretation: Users who found blockchain useful also found it easier to use, consistent with TAM assumptions.

**4. Logistic Regression Analysis**  
- Tested influence of PU and PEU on likelihood of agreeing that blockchain improves ECM.  
- Model accuracy: ~84.7%.  
- Supported both hypotheses (H1: PU → Adoption; H2: PEU → Adoption).

**5. Model Assumptions Testing**  
- Shapiro-Wilk Test: Confirmed non-normality, suitable for logistic modeling.  
- Multicollinearity Check (VIF): No serious multicollinearity among predictors.

## Key Findings
- **Positive Adoption Intent:** Users reported favorable perceptions of both usefulness and ease of use for a blockchain-enabled ECM platform.  
- **Workflow Management Gap:** Survey responses highlight the workflow-management function as an area requiring further technical exploration.

## Conclusions & Future Work
- **Conclusions:**  
  - Customer perceptions of PU and PEU strongly influence adoption intentions for blockchain-enabled ECM.  
  - High reliability and significant correlations affirm TAM’s applicability in enterprise technology adoption studies.

- **Future Work:**  
  - Enhance workflow-management capabilities within blockchain-enabled ECM solutions.  
  - Explore longitudinal studies to track adoption over time.
